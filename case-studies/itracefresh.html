The Challenge: Turning Fragmented Traceability Tools Into a Reason to Stay

iTracefresh had valuable traceability capabilities, but customers experienced them as disconnected tools. The most-used workflow—creating labels—was slow to learn and costly to operate, while lower-priced competitors offered a more coherent end-to-end product.

01

A weak product proposition

Customers could find cheaper alternatives, while iTracefresh’s stronger capabilities felt scattered across separate modules. This made it difficult for customers to understand the value of paying more.

02

A difficult core workflow

Creating labels was the most-used workflow, yet it was slow, error-prone, and difficult for new users to learn. This friction affected onboarding, daily operations, and support.

03

A constrained redesign

The redesign needed to preserve operational familiarity, meet PTI and FSMA traceability requirements, and avoid disrupting active work for growers and packing crews.

02

My Path For Success

Research in the Field

In The Field With Harvest Crews

Spent a day on-site at a California Giant Berry Farm observing and interviewing harvest and operations teams to understand the real-world workflow. Because subject-matter expert time was limited, I used field observation, focused interviews, and workflow mapping to make each research session count.

Who We Were Designing For

Our customers' pain points were our opportunities. Our interviews helped us uncover the path to our goals.

What The Journey Mapping Revealed

Workflow mapping revealed two major time sinks:

  • 5 hrs — Packing & prepping product
  • 1.5 hrs — Palletizing & labeling

These tasks consumed 6.5 hours of the shift, exposing significant workflow friction. A lack of real-time visibility left crews guessing about supplies, progress, and quotas—while operations teams lacked timely insight into production and quality issues.

What The Competitive Landscape Confirmed

iTracefresh wasn't behind on capability. It held features most competitors didn't but those solutions were scattered across separate, disconnected modules, while competitors like Procurant and Famous ERP won on being one unified system.

Designing One Connected Traceability Experience

Research showed that iTracefresh did not lack capability; it lacked a connected experience. I focused the redesign on making core labeling faster, bringing related workflows into one product structure, and giving growers clearer tools to manage customer engagement and recall events.

01

Unify the product structure

Research signalCustomers had to move between disconnected modules to complete related tasks.
Design decisionSo I unified the navigation to make the platform easier to find and understand.
02

Make label creation the core workflow

Research signalLabel creation was the most-used but most frustrating workflow.
Design decisionSo I introduced a guided wizard that reduced clicks while preserving the steps experienced users already understood.
03

Extend value beyond compliance

Research signalGrowers needed more than compliance labeling.
Design decisionSo I connected brand and recall tools to the same traceability data, creating more value without disrupting the core workflow.

What This Project Taught Me

Redesigning iTracefresh taught me that improving an operational product required more than modernizing its interface. I needed to understand the work in context, prioritize the workflow with the greatest customer impact, and connect existing capabilities into a more coherent product.

01
02
03
04

  1. Observe the work where it happens

    Watching harvest and operations teams in the field showed me that labeling was only one step within a time-sensitive physical workflow. Seeing that environment helped me design around real constraints instead of assumptions.

  2. Improve the core workflow first

    Label creation was the most-used feature and a major source of customer frustration. Prioritizing and validating that workflow gave the broader product redesign a measurable foundation.

  3. Modernize without disrupting expertise

    Experienced crews already understood the concepts and sequence behind label creation. The redesign added guidance, visible progress, and automation while preserving the familiar structure users relied on.

  4. Product value comes from connection

    iTracefresh already had strong capabilities, but they were scattered across separate modules. Connecting labeling, brand engagement, and recall response around shared traceability data created a clearer and more valuable product experience.
